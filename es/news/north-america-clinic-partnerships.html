Building clinic partnerships in North America: what actually converts

Lessons from clinic start-up projects on the difference between a supplier relationship and a partnership that survives year two.

AWA Business Development 12 de noviembre de 2025 7 min read

The first year of a clinic supply relationship is usually straightforward: equipment ships, herbs arrive, training happens. The relationship is tested in year two, when the initial enthusiasm has passed and the practice is dealing with the operational reality of a TCM service line.

Four things that separate partnerships that last

  • Documentation that satisfies the local regulator without a scramble. Clinics rarely have a regulatory specialist on staff, so the supplier effectively becomes the compliance department.
  • Replenishment predictability. A granule cabinet running empty mid-week costs more in lost bookings than the inventory would have cost to hold.
  • Clinical support that answers within a day. Practitioners escalate the unusual cases, and a slow answer erodes confidence in the whole relationship.
  • A named human. Shared inboxes and ticket queues generate churn; a known account manager with a direct channel does not.

What we changed after the first cohort

Three changes came directly from partner feedback. We moved from catalogue ordering to stock-plan agreements, so partners hold a planned inventory with scheduled replenishment. We added a practitioner advice line with a same-business-day response commitment. And we began co-branding patient-facing materials, because practitioners told us that generic handouts undercut their professional presentation.

If you are planning a clinic launch, the most useful first conversation is not about equipment lists. It is about your patient flow, your regulatory context and your staffing model. The equipment decision is much easier once those three are settled.
