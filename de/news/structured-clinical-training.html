Why structured clinical training beats intensive short courses

Contact hours are easy to sell and hard to defend. A tiered curriculum with supervised practice produces practitioners who stay in practice.

AWA Clinical Education 2. Oktober 2025 5 min read

A two-week intensive course can teach a great deal, but it cannot produce a confident practitioner. The gap is not knowledge; it is supervised repetition on real patients with feedback while the decision is still reversible.

The three-tier structure

We deliver foundations, supervised practice and advanced case management as separate tiers with separate assessments. Participants are not advanced on attendance. Tier 2 requires logged supervised hours and a case portfolio review before Tier 3 begins, which means a participant who is not ready is told so while the stakes are still low.

What the portfolio contains

  • Twenty documented cases with presenting pattern, treatment principle, formula and outcome at follow-up.
  • Two cases that did not go as expected, with an honest retrospective.
  • Supervisor sign-off on needling technique and safety procedures.
  • A reflective statement on scope of practice in the participant’s own jurisdiction.

The uncomfortable cases are mandatory. Practitioners who only present their successes have not been trained to handle the situations that generate complaints.

Alignment with local requirements

Continuing-education requirements differ substantially between jurisdictions, and we map the curriculum against the participant’s own registration body before the cohort starts, so the documented hours count where they need to count.
